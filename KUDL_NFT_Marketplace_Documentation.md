## 1. Overview

The document describes KUDL NFT as an NFT marketplace based on
algorithmic trading. It says the platform uses automatic algorithmic
quotations and automatic liquidity maintenance. It contrasts this model
with traditional NFT marketplace order-book or quotation models, which
the document says may be affected by market sentiment and periods of low
liquidity.

The document's central claim is that its algorithmic pricing model is
intended to provide liquidity for each NFT asset.

> The "world's first" description and statements about liquidity,
> pricing, returns, security, and performance are claims made in the
> supplied project document, not independently verified facts.

## 2. Introduction

### 2.1 What KUDL NFT is

KUDL NFT is described as an encrypted NFT integrated marketplace based
on algorithmic trading. The document compares its intended model with
traditional NFT marketplaces such as OpenSea, LooksRare, and X2Y2.

### 2.2 How the model is described

According to the document:

-   Traditional NFT prices can fluctuate due to market sentiment and
    investor behaviour.
-   When a project's popularity decreases, an NFT may have limited
    liquidity.
-   KUDL NFT proposes automatic algorithmic quotations.
-   The document says prices may automatically rise and liquidity may be
    automatically maintained through its algorithmic model.

### 2.3 Project vision

The document describes a native NFT trading platform and a
Metaverse-based earnings platform. It says earnings and profits
generated through algorithmic trading are shared with participants
through decentralization and community incentives. These are
project-document claims; no guarantee of earnings is established by this
Markdown conversion.

## 3. Background and NFT Liquidity Challenges

The document identifies liquidity and price discovery as important
requirements in NFT trading.

It describes the following challenges:

1.  **Few buyers:** Each NFT can be unique, so its pool of potential
    buyers may be smaller than the market for fungible tokens.
2.  **Difficult pricing:** NFTs may have limited historical data and no
    universally accepted valuation method. Items in the same collection
    may have different values because of rarity and subjective
    preferences.
3.  **High prices:** The document gives a historical blue-chip NFT price
    range of USD 11,000--120,000 and says high entry costs can
    discourage some participants.

The document says KUDL NFT was designed in response to these liquidity
and pricing challenges through an automatic algorithmic quotation model.

## 4. Platform and Pooling Algorithm Model

### 4.1 Pooling Algorithm Model

The document calls the platform's core trading algorithm the **Pooling
Algorithm Model**. It describes the model as centralizing NFT trades
into pools rather than requiring every buyer to match directly with one
seller.

### 4.2 Claimed characteristics

-   **Pooling trades:** Buyers and sellers interact with a pool of NFTs
    rather than relying only on a direct buyer--seller match.
-   **Instant settlement:** The document says participants receive trade
    results immediately after participation is decided.
-   **Trading speed and efficiency:** Centralized pooling is presented
    as a way to reduce waiting for a specific counterparty.
-   **Liquidity:** The document says pooling can attract more
    participants and allow more NFTs to circulate in the pools.

These are descriptions and claims from the source document. Actual
operation would need to be confirmed from deployed contracts, product
behaviour, and technical audits.

## 5. Automatic Pricing and Fractional Transactions

## 18. System Architecture

### 18.1 Hybrid architecture

The document describes a hybrid off-chain/on-chain exchange. It says
taker orders are matched with maker orders to execute NFT transactions.

It describes the platform as combining blockchain technology with NFT
asset circulation and says the architecture is intended to support
high-processing-capacity, low-latency data services for smart contracts,
scalability, recovery, and upgrades.

### 18.2 Non-custodial exchange

The source describes the exchange as non-custodial. It says orders
require approval for fungible and non-fungible tokens to be transferred
through the exchange.

### 18.3 Maker orders and transactions

-   Off-chain maker orders are described as EIP-712 signatures stored
    off-chain.
-   A transaction is described as exchanging a specified amount of
    ERC-20 tokens for NFT tokens, such as ERC-721 or ERC-1155, at a
    specified price.
-   The document also mentions traditional cryptocurrency exchange and
    transaction fees that may be used as agreement fees and/or
    royalties.

### 18.4 Core architecture components

-   **Currency Manager:** Handles the whitelist of ERC-20 tokens valid
    for transactions.
-   **Execution Manager:** Handles valid execution policies for matching
    makers and acceptors.
-   **Transfer Selector / NFT selector (RoyaltyFeeManager):** Provides
    logic for collecting royalties.
-   **RoyaltyFeeRegistry:** The document says this supports an
    independent royalty registry.
-   **ERC-2981:** The document says the architecture supports the NFT
    royalty standard and plans to support third-party royalty-fee
    oracles.

### 18.5 System features claimed in the source

-   **High throughput:** The document claims second-level confirmation
    and strong transaction throughput.
-   **Interoperability:** It describes cross-chain data services and
    transfers between multiple chains and NFT categories.
-   **Liquidity:** It says users can participate in global NFT
    circulation.
-   **Convenience:** It mentions services and APIs for NFT blockchain
    applications.

These statements describe the proposed or claimed architecture;
deployment and performance are not verified by the source PDF.
